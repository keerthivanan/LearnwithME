### Q1: "How would you approach a discovery workshop with a new client who wants to implement Edrevel for compliance training?"

**Model Answer:**
> "I'd structure it in 3 phases:
>
> **Phase 1 — Current State (AS-IS):** 'Walk me through how compliance training works today.'
> I want to understand: How do employees know they need to complete training? How is completion tracked? What happens when someone misses a deadline? Where does the data live?
>
> **Phase 2 — Pain Points:** 'What's broken or frustrating?' Typically I hear: manual tracking in Excel, managers chasing people, audit panic, no visibility into who's actually competent vs. just 'completed'.
>
> **Phase 3 — Success Definition:** 'What does great look like in 12 months?' Usually: automated reminders, real-time dashboard for compliance officers, audit-ready reports in 1 click, certificates auto-issued.
>
> Then I'd map each pain point to a specific Edrevel module — and flag any gaps that need custom configuration. I'd close with a prioritized roadmap: what we implement first, what comes in phase 2.
>
> I always document this as a Solution Brief that the client can share internally — it shows I understood them and builds trust."

### Q2: "How would you explain Edrevel's AI Course Creator to a CFO who is skeptical about AI?"

**Model Answer:**
> "I wouldn't start with AI. I'd start with pain.
>
> 'Right now, your L&D team takes 6–8 weeks to build one compliance course. Regulations change every year — that means constant rebuilding. Your team is a bottleneck.
>
> What if they could generate a complete, structured course on GDPR updates in under an hour? With quizzes, learning objectives, and assessments already built? Then spend their time on what humans do best — customizing tone, adding real company examples, reviewing for accuracy.
>
> That's what AI Course Creator does. It's not replacing your team. It's removing the grunt work so they can focus on quality and strategy.
>
> And the ROI math is straightforward: if one L&D person spends 80% of their time building content today, and AI handles 70% of that work — you've just freed up significant capacity without adding headcount.'"

### Q3: "What's the difference between an LMS and an LXP, and why does it matter for clients?"

**Model Answer:**
> "An LMS — Learning Management System — is built around administration. An admin assigns courses, employees complete them, the system tracks compliance. It's top-down, mandatory, and honestly, learners often dread it.
>
> An LXP — Learning Experience Platform — flips that model. It's learner-driven. Think Netflix vs. a mandatory reading list. The platform recommends content based on your role, your goals, your skill gaps. Learning feels relevant instead of obligatory.
>
> For a client, this matters because engagement is the #1 problem in corporate learning. You can have perfect compliance content, but if no one engages with it voluntarily, your completion rates are driven purely by deadlines and threats.
>
> Edrevel does both — it handles the compliance mandate side (LMS features) but wraps it in an LXP experience (personalization, skill tracking, discovery). That's a meaningful differentiator because most competitors do one or the other."

### Q4: "A client's engagement data shows 20% active users. How would you diagnose and fix this?"

**Model Answer:**
> "20% active is a symptom — I'd dig into the data to find the cause before proposing a fix.
>
> **First, I'd segment the data:**
> - Are the 80% inactive evenly spread, or concentrated in specific departments/roles?
> - What's the device split? If content isn't mobile-optimized, that alone kills engagement.
> - When was the last content update? Stale content = learners don't come back.
> - Are there any notification/reminder flows configured?
>
> **Common root causes I've seen:**
> 1. Content is long, desktop-only, and feels like punishment — not relevant to daily work
> 2. No push notifications or reminders
> 3. Managers aren't championing learning — it's seen as optional
> 4. The homepage isn't personalized — everyone sees the same 100 courses
>
> **Proposed fix (tied to findings):**
> - Re-format top 10 most important courses for mobile
> - Set up automated weekly nudges: 'You're 15 minutes away from completing [skill]'
> - Activate the personalized homepage so each user sees their recommended next step
> - Create a manager dashboard so team leads can see their team's learning activity
>
> Set a 30-day re-measurement checkpoint so the client can see the impact of each change."

### Q5: "How do you prioritize features when a client requests something not on the product roadmap?"

**Model Answer:**
> "I use a 3-step approach:
>
> **1. Understand the root need** — 9 times out of 10, the feature request is a symptom. 'We need to bulk-assign courses based on a CSV upload' usually means 'our HR system doesn't integrate with you yet'. Understanding the real need opens up alternative solutions.
>
> **2. Explore current capabilities** — Before escalating to product, I check if there's an existing way to solve it. Maybe it's a configuration option they haven't discovered. Maybe it requires a workaround that's slightly manual but good enough for now.
>
> **3. Document and escalate strategically** — If it's a genuine gap, I write it up as a product request with business impact: 'This client represents $X ARR, this request has come from 3 other clients in the last 2 months, here's the use case.' That's what gets product teams' attention — patterns across customers, not one-off requests.
>
> I always set clear expectations with the client: 'This is not available today. Here's a workaround. I've submitted it as a priority request — here's what the process looks like.'"

### Q6: "How would you build a business case / proposal for a mid-size company to adopt Edrevel?"

```

6. NEXT STEPS
   - Specific: "Sign agreement by [date] → kickoff call scheduled for [date]"
```

### Q7: "What do you know about current AI trends in EdTech?"

**Key trends to mention:**
1. **Generative AI for content creation** — LLMs building courses from a prompt or PDF
2. **AI tutors / chatbots** — 24/7 learning assistants inside the platform
3. **Skills inference** — AI infers skills from job titles, completed courses, even LinkedIn profiles (no manual tagging)
4. **Multimodal learning** — AI converting text → video, audio, interactive simulations
5. **Real-time coaching** — AI analyzing sales call recordings or customer interactions → feedback
6. **Compliance automation** — AI monitoring regulatory changes → auto-updating courses

> "The exciting shift is from 'AI as content generator' to 'AI as learning coach' — platforms are moving toward real-time, contextual guidance rather than one-time course delivery. Edrevel's SkillsPro engine is positioned right in that direction."
