# Questions to Ask — Happy Robots Interview

---

## Why This Matters at Manager Level

Your questions signal your depth. Weak candidates ask generic questions. You ask questions that show you've read the JD carefully, you understand production AI challenges, and you're thinking about how to succeed in the role — not just get it.

Rule: Ask at least 3. Have 6 ready.

---

## Tier 1: Ask These First (Highest Impact)

---

**"The JD mentions operating across voice, email, SMS, and chat channels. What's the current maturity of the voice channel specifically — is it in active production deployments, or is it a roadmap capability?"**

*Why it's good:* Voice AI is a specific skill they've called out. This tests whether they actually have it live or are aspirational. Their answer tells you what skills you'll use immediately vs. what you'll be building from scratch.

---

**"How does the team currently approach prompt and configuration version control? Is there a CI/CD pipeline for AI Worker deployments, or is it more manual today?"**

*Why it's good:* This is a specific MLOps maturity question from the JD. Their answer reveals whether they have engineering rigour or are still in 'edit in the UI and hope it works' territory. A strong team will have a clear answer; an immature practice will be vague.

---

**"The role mentions ensuring Responsible AI and compliance — are there specific regulatory frameworks or industry compliance requirements (GDPR, HIPAA, India DPDP Act, industry certifications) that active deployments are already governed by?"**

*Why it's good:* Shows you know Responsible AI is more than a buzzword — it has regulatory teeth. Their answer tells you the compliance maturity of their client base and how real the governance work is.

---

**"What does the BUIT relationship look like in practice — are BUIT teams generally clear about requirements coming in, or is requirements clarification a significant part of the work?"**

*Why it's good:* This is a JD-specific term. Using it shows you read the description carefully. Their answer tells you how structured the engagement model is and how much business analysis skill you'll need vs. pure configuration.

---

**"The JD mentions model cards, decision records, and audit logs. Is this documentation practice already standardized across deployments, or would the Manager role be partly responsible for establishing these standards?"**

*Why it's good:* Two possible answers: 'yes it's standardized' (you're stepping into a structured practice) or 'it's partly your job to establish it' (bigger scope, more ownership). Both are good for different reasons — you want to know which you're walking into.

---

## Tier 2: Technical Depth Questions

---

**"What LLMs are currently in production on the HappyRobots platform — are you primarily using OpenAI APIs, Azure OpenAI, Anthropic, or are there self-hosted models in use for privacy-sensitive clients?"**

*Why:* Reveals the tech stack you'll work with from day one. Also reveals data privacy maturity — self-hosted = they've thought about it; all cloud = may have constraints ahead.

---

**"How is token cost currently tracked and reported — is there a per-solution cost dashboard, and who owns cost optimization decisions?"**

*Why:* Cost management is explicitly in the JD. This shows you think about business impact. Their answer tells you how mature the cost governance is.

---

**"What data annotation and evaluation practices are currently in place? Do you have a standard evaluation dataset creation process, or is each deployment tested more ad-hoc?"**

*Why:* Evaluation maturity is a big quality differentiator. Their answer tells you how much quality infrastructure exists vs. how much you'll be building.

---

**"For document processing deployments — what OCR/pre-processing stack is being used for scanned documents, and have you encountered challenges with Indian document formats (GST invoices, multi-language documents)?"**

*Why:* Very specific to the India context and the JD's document processing focus. Shows you've thought about real-world document complexity in India.

---

## Tier 3: Team, Career & Culture

---

**"What does the team structure look like — how many people would I be managing or working with, and what's the mix of skills (Python engineers, platform configurators, data annotators)?"**

*Why:* Practical manager question. You need to know your team before day one.

---

**"What does success look like for this role at 6 months? If you were describing your best-case outcome for this hire to your leadership in 6 months, what would you say?"**

*Why:* Classic but important. Tells you the real priorities, not just the JD priorities.

---

**"Is the Chennai team a full product + engineering + delivery team, or more of a delivery centre for work scoped by another location?"**

*Why:* Autonomy and career ceiling depend on this. A delivery centre limits decision-making authority. A full team is much more valuable for your growth.

---

**"What are the biggest challenges the team is facing right now — technical, operational, or otherwise?"**

*Why:* Open-ended but powerful. Strong teams are honest about their challenges. The answer tells you what you're actually walking into.

---

## The Close — Use This to End Every Interview

After asking your questions, close with:

**"Based on our conversation today — is there anything about my background or approach that gives you pause about my fit for this role? I'd much rather address any questions directly than have them be an open question."**

*Why this works:*
- Shows confidence — only strong candidates ask this
- Gives you a chance to handle objections in real time
- Interviewers remember this question — it signals seriousness
- If they have a concern, you learn it now and can address it

---

## Questions to AVOID

| Avoid asking | Reason |
|-------------|--------|
| "What does Happy Robots do?" | You must know this already |
| "What's the salary / benefits?" | Too early in first round |
| "Is there remote work?" | Signals you'd rather not be there |
| "How long are typical work hours?" | Wrong priority signal for manager level |
| "What happens if the AI makes a mistake?" | Too vague — shows you haven't thought about production risks specifically |

---

## Taking Notes During Their Answers

Write down key phrases when they answer your questions:
- Names of specific tools they mention (for your follow-up or thank-you note)
- Challenges they describe (reference them in your thank-you: "I've faced X too, here's how I'd approach it")
- Mismatches between JD and their actual description of the role (address before you accept an offer)
